I started playing Sunflower Land in early 2025, mostly out of curiosity about what a browser-based blockchain game actually felt like to live with. A year later, I've built three farms, reset twice, and spent more time thinking about potato yield optimisation than I'd like to admit.

The interesting thing about Sunflower Land isn't the economy — it's the pacing. Most games are designed to pull you back constantly. Push notifications, daily login bonuses, limited-time events engineered to create anxiety about missing out. Sunflower Land is designed around waiting. You plant, you leave, you come back later. The game runs without you.

> The best games are the ones that teach you something about yourself. Usually something slightly embarrassing.

What it taught me: I like tending things. I like having a small world to look after. I like the particular satisfaction of a full barn and a clear plan for the morning. These are not things I would have said about myself before I had a pixel farm.

The economy is real — there are tokens, NFTs, a genuine market — but it recedes into the background once you've been playing for a few weeks. You stop thinking about the token price and start thinking about what you're going to plant next. That shift, from investor to farmer, is the moment the game actually starts.

The two resets were both voluntary, and both felt like the right decision at the time. The first because I'd made early choices that limited what I could build. The second because I'd optimised myself into a corner and forgotten why I was playing. Both times, starting over felt like loss for about a week, and then like freedom.

I don't know if I'll be playing a year from now. But I know it taught me something useful about patience, and about how much I like having small things to take care of.
